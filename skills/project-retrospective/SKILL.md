# Project retrospective

Identify a change worth keeping after a completed piece of work.

Use this workflow when the user selects it or specifically asks for this deliverable.

## Workflow

1. Build a timeline from artifacts, distinguishing the original information from hindsight.
2. Locate the decision point where a small intervention could have changed the outcome. Look for counterexamples in work that went well.
3. Propose one reversible experiment with an owner and a way to tell whether the next review improved.

## Try it

Three review rounds were needed because two terms meant different things.

**Expected result.** A dated observation, an evidence-backed hypothesis and a measurable next experiment.

Compare the original brief with the first conflicting comment. If both readers used the term correctly and a later scope change caused the extra review, a glossary would address the wrong problem. Otherwise try defining the disputed term in the next brief and count clarification rounds. State what result would lead the owner to drop the experiment.

**Limit.** Avoid attributing intent to people from timestamps alone.
