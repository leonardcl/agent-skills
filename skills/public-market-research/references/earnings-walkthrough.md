# Reconcile a reported result

Use the same fiscal quarter, accounting basis and currency for both actual and consensus. A result of 108 million against a 100 million estimate is an 8 percent surprise. Comparing 108 with 90 in the prior year instead measures 20 percent growth. If the forecast is absent or zero, show the amount difference where meaningful and leave the percentage undefined.

| Item | Quarter A | Quarter B |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Reported revenue, millions | 90 | 108 |
| Consensus for that quarter, millions | 95 | 100 |
| Shares at the reporting date, millions | 10 | 12 |

An increase in total revenue does not establish an increase per share. Both quarters have revenue of 9 per share on these illustrative ending share counts. For an earnings-per-share analysis, use the reported weighted-average share count and earnings definition instead.

When publishing an as-of note, record the release date and the last pre-release estimate timestamp separately. An estimate revised after publication cannot be the independent surprise baseline.
